### Overview

The zip is a complete Lake project. `lakefile.toml` and `lake-manifest.json` use the pinned Mathlib checkout at `/opt/mathlib4` (commit `9944fe2973b8dc0b86949101ed98232c07cd54a0`), and `lean-toolchain` is `leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1`. The proof is split into eight files under `TuringTask/` that import each other in a chain: `Fib`, `Rank`, `Psi`, `Size`, `Prim`, `Apps`, `Main`, `Basic`. The required file `TuringTask/Basic.lean` is the file of the prompt: `import Mathlib`, the namespace and the five statements are unchanged, and each `sorry` is replaced by a one-line call of the matching theorem in `Main.lean`.

Notation: $F_n$ is the Fibonacci number `Nat.fib n`, $L_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n+1} = 2F_{n+1} - F_n$ is the Lucas number, $\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $\psi = (1-\sqrt5)/2$ are the golden ratio and its conjugate (Mathlib's `goldenRatio`, `goldenConj`), $\Phi_n$ is the $n$-th cyclotomic polynomial, $\mu$ is the Moebius function and $v_p$ the $p$-adic valuation. A prime $q$ is primitive for $n$ if $q \mid F_n$ and $q \nmid F_k$ for $1 \le k < n$.

The proof is the classical argument for primitive divisors of Lucas sequences, carried out for the Fibonacci numbers:
- (A) every prime $p$ dividing some $F_k$ has a rank of apparition $r_p$ with $p \mid F_k \iff r_p \mid k$, and for odd $p$ the exponent satisfies $v_p(F_{r_p e}) = v_p(F_{r_p}) + v_p(e)$ (lifting the exponent); for $p = 2$ the exponent of $F_n$ is $0$, $1$ or $v_2(n) + 2$;
- (B) the primitive part $\Psi_n = \prod_{d \mid n} F_d^{\mu(n/d)}$ is a positive integer with $\prod_{d \mid n}\Psi_d = F_n$; by Moebius inversion of (A), a prime factor $p$ of $\Psi_n$ that divides an earlier Fibonacci number is unique, equals the largest prime factor of $n$ when odd (and forces $n = 3 \cdot 2^a$ when $p = 2$), and divides $\Psi_n$ exactly once, provided $n \ne 6, 12$;
- (C) $\Psi_n = \psi^{\varphi(n)}\Phi_n(\varphi/\psi)$ for $n \ge 2$, and the factorisation of $\Phi_n$ over $\mathbb{C}$ together with $\Phi_{mq}(z)\Phi_m(z) = \Phi_m(z^q)$ gives $\Psi_n > p$ for that prime $p$, and $\Psi_n > 1$ for every $n \ge 3$.

So for $n \ge 3$, $n \ne 6, 12$, the integer $\Psi_n$ has a prime factor that divides no earlier Fibonacci number (theorem 1). Theorems 2 to 5 are derived from theorem 1 and from (A).

### 1. Fib.lean: Fibonacci and Lucas identities

- `luc n` is the Lucas number as an integer, `luc_real` is Binet's formula $L_n = \varphi^n + \psi^n$ (from Mathlib's `goldenRatio_mul_fib_succ_add_fib` and its conjugate), `fib_real` is $\sqrt5 F_n = \varphi^n - \psi^n$ (`Real.coe_fib_eq`).
- `luc_sq_sub`: $L_n^2 - 5F_n^2 = 4(-1)^n$; `fib_two_mul_luc`: $F_{2n} = F_n L_n$ (`Nat.fib_two_mul`); `cassini`: $F_{n+2}F_n - F_{n+1}^2 = (-1)^{n+1}$ by induction.
- `fib_mul_expand`: with $F = F_{m+1}$ and $f = F_m$, for every $q$ there are integers $u, t$ with $F_{(q+2)m} = (q+2)F^{q+1}f - \binom{q+2}{2}F^q f^2 + f^3 u$ and $F_{(q+2)m+1} = F^{q+2} + f^2 t$. The proof is an induction on $q$ using the addition formula `Nat.fib_add` in the form $F_{(q+3)m} = F_{(q+2)m}(F - f) + F_{(q+2)m+1} f$ and $F_{(q+3)m+1} = F_{(q+2)m} f + F_{(q+2)m+1} F$. `fib_mul_expand'` is the first-order version $F_{em} = eF^{e-1}f + f^2 s$ for all $e \ge 1$.
- `oddSum n` is $T_n = \sum_{k \text{ odd}} \binom{n}{k} 5^{(k-1)/2}$; `two_pow_mul_fib`: $2^n F_n = 2T_n$, obtained from $(1+\sqrt5)^n - (1-\sqrt5)^n = 2^n\sqrt5\,F_n$ and the binomial theorem. For an odd prime $q$ only the term $k = q$ survives modulo $q$ (`oddSum_prime_modEq`: $T_q \equiv 5^{(q-1)/2}$), so with Fermat's little theorem `fib_sq_eq_one`: $F_q^2 \equiv 1 \pmod q$ for every prime $q \ne 5$.
- `prime_dvd_fib_pred_or_succ`: Cassini at $n = q - 1$ gives $F_{q+1}F_{q-1} \equiv F_q^2 + (-1)^q \equiv 0$, so $q \mid F_{q-1}$ or $q \mid F_{q+1}$ for every prime $q \ne 5$.

### 2. Rank.lean: the rank of apparition and the exponents

- `HasRank p` says that $p$ divides some $F_k$ with $k \ge 1$; `rk p h` is the least such $k$ (`Nat.find`). `dvd_fib_iff`: $p \mid F_k \iff r_p \mid k$, from `Nat.fib_gcd` and minimality. `rk_eq_of_primitive`: a primitive prime of $F_n$ has rank $n$. `rk_two`, `rk_three`, `rk_five`: the ranks of $2, 3, 5$ are $3, 4, 5$.
- `rk_dvd_pred_or_succ`: $r_p \mid p - 1$ or $r_p \mid p + 1$ for $p \ne 5$; `prime_dvd_rk_le`: every prime factor of $r_p$ is at most $p$ when $p$ is odd (a prime factor $\ell = p + 1$ would be even).
- `padicValNat_fib_mul_of_not_dvd`: if $p \mid F_m$ and $p \nmid e$ then $v_p(F_{em}) = v_p(F_m)$, because $F_{em}/F_m \equiv eF_{m+1}^{e-1} \pmod{F_m}$ and $p \nmid F_{m+1}$ (`Nat.fib_coprime_fib_succ`). `padicValNat_fib_mul_self`: for odd $p \mid F_m$, $v_p(F_{pm}) = v_p(F_m) + 1$, because $F_{pm}/F_m = pF_{m+1}^{p-1} - \binom p2 F_{m+1}^{p-2}F_m + F_m^2 u$ is divisible by $p$ but not by $p^2$. Together (`padicValNat_fib_rk_mul`, `padicValNat_fib_eq`): $v_p(F_d) = v_p(F_{r_p}) + v_p(d/r_p)$ if $r_p \mid d$, and $0$ otherwise.
- The prime 2: `lucN n` is $L_n$ as a natural number, `fib_two_mul_lucN`, `lucN_sq`. From $L_m^2 = 5F_m^2 + 4(-1)^m$: if $m$ is odd with $3 \mid m$ then $L_m^2 = 16(5a(a+1)+1)$ with $F_m = 2(2a+1)$, so $v_2(L_m) = 2$; if $6 \mid m$ then $L_m^2 = 4(80w^2 + 1)$ with $F_m = 8w$, so $v_2(L_m) = 1$ (`padicValNat_two_of_sq_eq_sixteen_mul`, `padicValNat_two_of_sq_eq_four_mul`). `padicValNat_two_fib_three_mul_odd`: $v_2(F_{3e}) = 1$ for odd $e$, from `fib_mul_expand'`. A strong induction (`padicValNat_two_fib`) then gives $v_2(F_n) = 0$ if $3 \nmid n$, $1$ if $n \equiv 3 \pmod 6$, and $v_2(n) + 2$ if $6 \mid n$.

### 3. Psi.lean: the primitive part

- `psi n` is $\prod_{(a,b),\ ab = n} F_b^{\mu(a)}$ as a rational number (`Nat.divisorsAntidiagonal`, integer powers). `psi_pos`; `prod_psi`: $\prod_{d \mid n}\psi_d = F_n$ by Mathlib's Moebius inversion `prod_eq_iff_prod_pow_moebius_eq_of_nonzero`.
- `padicValRat_psi`: $v_p(\psi_n) = \sum_{ab = n}\mu(a)\,v_p(F_b)$ (`padicValRat.mul`, `padicValRat.zpow`).
- Three Moebius sums over the antidiagonal of $m$: `moebius_sum_one` ($\sum\mu(a) = [m = 1]$), `moebius_sum_lam` ($\sum \mu(a) v_p(b) = \Lambda_p(m)$, where `lam p m` is $1$ if $m$ is a positive power of $p$ and $0$ otherwise; this is Moebius inversion of $v_p(m) = \#\{j \ge 1 : p^j \mid m\}$, `sum_lam`) and `moebius_sum_two` ($\sum \mu(a)[2 \mid b] = [m = 2]$), all from `sum_eq_iff_sum_mul_moebius_eq`.
- `moebius_sum_rank`: for a weight supported on the multiples of $r$, $\sum_{ab = rm}\mu(a)\,[r \mid b]\,(c + v_p(b/r) + \kappa[2 \mid b/r]) = c[m = 1] + \Lambda_p(m) + \kappa[m = 2]$; the sum is reindexed by $b = re$ with $e \mid m$.
- `padicValRat_psi_odd`: for an odd prime $p$ of rank $r$, $v_p(\psi_n) = v_p(F_r)[n = r] + \Lambda_p(n/r)$ if $r \mid n$, and $0$ otherwise. `padicValRat_psi_two`: $v_2(\psi_n) = [n = 3] + \Lambda_2(n/3) + [n = 6]$ if $3 \mid n$, and $0$ otherwise. Both follow from the exponent formulas of `Rank.lean` and `moebius_sum_rank`.
- `padicValRat_psi_nonneg`, `psi_den_eq_one`: every valuation is nonnegative, so the denominator of $\psi_n$ is $1$ (a prime factor of the denominator would give a negative valuation). `Psi n` is $\psi_n$ as a natural number; `Psi_cast`, `Psi_pos`, `prod_Psi`, `Psi_dvd_fib` ($\Psi_d \mid F_n$ for $d \mid n$).
- `nonprim_odd`: if an odd prime $p$ divides $\Psi_n$ and some $F_k$ with $1 \le k < n$, then $n = r_p p^j$ with $j \ge 1$ and $p^2 \nmid \Psi_n$ (the valuation is $\Lambda_p(n/r_p) = 1$). `nonprim_two`: if $2$ divides $\Psi_n$ and an earlier $F_k$, then $n = 3 \cdot 2^a$ with $a \ge 1$, and $4 \nmid \Psi_n$ unless $a = 1$.

### 4. Size.lean: the size bound

- `hcyc m X Y` is the homogeneous value $Y^{\varphi(m)}\Phi_m(X/Y)$ for real $X, Y$. `abs_cyclotomic_eval_ge`, `abs_cyclotomic_eval_le`: $(|x| - 1)^{\varphi(m)} \le |\Phi_m(x)| \le (|x| + 1)^{\varphi(m)}$ for real $x$ with $|x| \ge 1$, from $\Phi_m(z) = \prod_\zeta (z - \zeta)$ over the primitive $m$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb{C}$ (`cyclotomic_eq_prod_X_sub_primitiveRoots`, `Complex.isPrimitiveRoot_exp`, `IsPrimitiveRoot.norm'_eq_one`, `Complex.card_primitiveRoots`). Hence `abs_hcyc_ge`, `abs_hcyc_le`: $(|X| - |Y|)^{\varphi(m)} \le |\mathrm{hcyc}_m(X,Y)| \le (|X| + |Y|)^{\varphi(m)}$.
- `hcyc_mul_prime`: $\mathrm{hcyc}_{mq}(X,Y)\,\mathrm{hcyc}_m(X,Y) = \mathrm{hcyc}_m(X^q,Y^q)$ for a prime $q \nmid m$ (`cyclotomic_expand_eq_cyclotomic_mul`, `expand_eval`, `Nat.totient_mul`). `hcyc_prime_pow`: $\mathrm{hcyc}_{p^{k+1}m}(X,Y) = \mathrm{hcyc}_{pm}(X^{p^k},Y^{p^k})$ (`cyclotomic_expand_eq_cyclotomic`, `Nat.totient_mul_of_prime_of_dvd`).
- `prod_hcyc_golden`: $\prod_{d \mid n}\mathrm{hcyc}_d(\varphi,\psi) = \varphi^n - \psi^n$ (`prod_cyclotomic_eq_X_pow_sub_one`, `Nat.sum_totient`); `hcyc_golden_ne_zero`. Moebius inversion in $\mathbb{R}^\times$ then gives `psi_eq_hcyc`: $\psi_n = \mathrm{hcyc}_n(\varphi,\psi)$ for $n \ge 2$, because $\prod_{ab = n}(\sqrt5)^{\mu(a)} = 1$ for $n \ge 2$.
- Real inequalities: `ineq_lin` and `ineq_const` are inductions on $q$; the bases use the exact values $\varphi^3 = 2\varphi + 1$, $\varphi^7 = 13\varphi + 8$, $\varphi^{-1} = \varphi - 1$, $\varphi^{-3} = 2\varphi - 3$, $\varphi^{-7} = 13\varphi - 21$ (`phi_pow_three`, `phi_pow_seven`, `phi_inv_pow_three`, `phi_inv_pow_seven`) and, for $a \ge \varphi^2 > 2.6$, elementary estimates (`ineq_big_lin`, `ineq_big_const`). Results: `ineq_phi_seven` ($q(\varphi + \varphi^{-1}) < \varphi^q - \varphi^{-q}$ for $q \ge 7$), `ineq_phi_three` ($\varphi + \varphi^{-1} < \varphi^q - \varphi^{-q}$ for $q \ge 3$), and the versions with $\varphi^t$, $t \ge 2$, in place of $\varphi$.
- `size_aux`, `size_core`: with $A = \varphi^{q^k}$, $B = \psi^{q^k}$ and $c \ge 1$, the inequality $c(|A| + |B|) < |A|^q - |B|^q$ gives $c\,|\mathrm{hcyc}_m(A,B)| \le (c(|A|+|B|))^{\varphi(m)} < (|A|^q - |B|^q)^{\varphi(m)} \le |\mathrm{hcyc}_m(A^q,B^q)| = |\mathrm{hcyc}_{qm}(A,B)|\,|\mathrm{hcyc}_m(A,B)|$, hence $c < \psi_{q^{k+1}m}$. `psi_gt_prime`: $\psi_{q^{k+1}m} > q$ when $q \ge 7$ or $k \ge 1$; `psi_gt_one`: $\psi_{q^{k+1}m} > 1$ when $q \ge 3$ or $k \ge 1$ (for a prime $q \nmid m$).

### 5. Prim.lean: the primitive divisor theorem

- `nonprim_unique`: for $n \ne 12$, two prime factors of $\Psi_n$ that both divide earlier Fibonacci numbers are equal. An odd such prime is the largest prime factor of $n$ (`prime_factor_le`), so two odd ones coincide; the prime $2$ forces $n = 3 \cdot 2^a$, so an odd companion would be $3$, and $4 \cdot 3^j = 3 \cdot 2^a$ forces $n = 12$.
- `nonprim_lt_Psi`: for $n \ge 3$, $n \ne 6, 12$, such a prime $p$ satisfies $p < \Psi_n$ and $p^2 \nmid \Psi_n$. The size bound is applied with $n = p^{k+1} m$, $p \nmid m$: for $p = 2$ ($n = 3 \cdot 2^a$, $a \ge 2$ since $n \ne 6$), $p = 5$ ($n = 5^{j+1}$), $p = 3$ ($n = 4 \cdot 3^j$, $j \ge 2$ since $n \ne 12$) the exponent $k \ge 1$; for $p \ge 7$ `ineq_phi_seven` applies.
- `two_le_Psi`: $\Psi_n \ge 2$ for $n \ge 3$ (an odd prime factor of $n$, or $n = 2^a$ with $a \ge 2$, together with `psi_gt_one`).
- `exists_primitive_prime`: take a prime $p \mid \Psi_n$. If $p$ divides no earlier $F_k$ it is primitive. Otherwise $\Psi_n = pM$ with $M \ge 2$ and $p \nmid M$; a prime factor $q$ of $M$ is different from $p$ and, by `nonprim_unique`, divides no earlier Fibonacci number. In both cases $q \mid \Psi_n \mid F_n$.

### 6. Apps.lean: the consequences

- `fib_pred_add_fib_succ`: $F_{n-1} + F_{n+1} = L_n$ for $n \ge 1$. `lucas_primitive`: for $n \ge 2$, $n \ne 6$, a primitive prime $q$ of $F_{2n}$ ($2n \ne 6, 12$ when $n \ne 3, 6$) divides $F_n L_n$ but not $F_n$, so $q \mid L_n$; and $q \mid L_k$ with $k < n$ would give $q \mid F_{2k}$. The case $n = 3$ ($L_3 = 4$) is checked directly with $q = 2$.
- `primPrime d` chooses a primitive prime of $F_d$ for $d \ge 3$, $d \ne 6, 12$; `primPrime_inj`: distinct such $d$ have distinct primitive primes (both would have rank equal to $d$). `card_divisors_le_card_primeFactors`: the divisors of $n$ outside $\{1, 2, 6, 12\}$ inject into the prime factors of $F_n$, so $\#\mathrm{divisors}(n) \le \omega(F_n) + 4$.
- `primeFactors_fib_eq_iff`: if $3 \le m < n$ and $F_m$, $F_n$ have the same prime factors, then $n \in \{6, 12\}$ (otherwise a primitive prime of $F_n$ would divide $F_m$), and the finitely many cases $n = 6$, $3 \le m \le 5$ and $n = 12$, $3 \le m \le 11$ are checked with the values $F_3, \dots, F_{12}$ (`fib_values`): only $(m, n) = (3, 6)$ survives.
- `exists_primitive_prime_mod`: a primitive prime $q$ of $F_n$ has rank $n$, so $q \ne 5$ when $n \ne 5$, and `rk_dvd_pred_or_succ` gives $n \mid q - 1$ or $n \mid q + 1$, i.e. $q \bmod n = 1$ or $q \bmod n = n - 1$ (`mod_eq_one_of_dvd_sub_one`, `mod_eq_sub_one_of_dvd_add_one`).

### 7. Main.lean and Basic.lean

`Main.lean` restates the five theorems of the prompt (`fib_primitive`, `lucas_primitive_mod`, `divisors_card_le`, `primeFactors_eq`, `primitive_mod`), converting between `x % q = 0` and `q | x` with `Nat.mod_eq_zero_of_dvd` and `Nat.dvd_of_mod_eq_zero`. `Basic.lean` is the prompt's file with each `sorry` replaced by `exact` and the corresponding theorem of `Main.lean`.
