### Overview

The zip is a complete Lake project. `lakefile.toml` and `lake-manifest.json` use the pinned Mathlib checkout at `/opt/mathlib4` (commit `9944fe2973b8dc0b86949101ed98232c07cd54a0`), and `lean-toolchain` is `leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1`. The proof sits in eight files under `TuringTask/` that import each other in a chain: `Dedekind`, `Formulas`, `Reciprocity`, `ModEight`, `Criteria`, `Knopp`, `Main`, `Basic`. Every file starts with `import Mathlib`. The required file `TuringTask/Basic.lean` is the file of the prompt with one added line `import TuringTask.Main`. Its namespace and its five statements stay as in the prompt, and each `sorry` became a one-line `exact` of the matching theorem in `Main.lean`.

In this text $((x))$ is the sawtooth function, $x - \lfloor x \rfloor - 1/2$ for $x \notin \mathbb{Z}$ and $0$ for $x \in \mathbb{Z}$ (Lean: `saw`). The hypothesis `hs` says that $s(h, k) = \sum_{r < k} ((r/k))((hr/k))$ (`dsum_eq`), and the Lean code calls this sum `dsum h k`. For coprime $h, k$ we write $\rho(r) = hr \bmod k$, $q(r) = \lfloor hr/k \rfloor$, $S_2 = \sum_{r<k} r\,q(r)$, $Q = \sum_{r<k} q(r)^2$ and $\Sigma q = \sum_{r<k} q(r)$.

The route:
- (A) Basic rules: period $1$ and oddness of $((x))$, the multiplication formula $\sum_{j<d} ((x + j/d)) = ((dx))$, and from them $s(h, k) = s(h \bmod k, k)$, $s(k - h, k) = -s(h, k)$, $s(h', k) = s(h, k)$ for $hh' \equiv 1 \pmod k$, and $s(dh, dk) = s(h, k)$.
- (B) For coprime $h, k$ the map $\rho$ permutes $\{0, \dots, k-1\}$. Comparing $\sum \rho$ and $\sum \rho^2$ with $\sum r$ and $\sum r^2$ gives integer formulas for $12 k s(h, k)$ and the congruence $12hk\,s(h, k) \equiv h^2 + 1 \pmod k$. These give theorems 2, 4 and 5.
- (C) Counting lattice points gives the reciprocity law, and induction on the odd modulus with Jacobi reciprocity gives theorem 3.
- (D) For theorem 1 the sum over $b$ collapses by the multiplication formula, Moebius inversion removes a coprimality condition, and the divisor sums telescope to $\sigma(n) s(h, k)$.

### 1. Dedekind.lean: the sawtooth function and the basic rules

- `saw_add_int`, `saw_neg`, `saw_natCast_div`: $((x + z)) = ((x))$ for $z \in \mathbb{Z}$, $((-x)) = -((x))$, and $((m/y)) = (m \bmod y)/y - 1/2$ unless $y \mid m$ (`Int.fract_div_natCast_eq_div_natCast_mod`).
- `saw_mul_sum`: the multiplication formula. The difference $D(x)$ of its two sides satisfies $D(x + 1/d) = D(x)$ (shift $j$ by one and use period $1$), and $D = 0$ on $[0, 1/d)$, where every term is explicit. Every $x$ is $x_0 + n/d$ with $x_0 \in [0, 1/d)$ and $n = \lfloor dx \rfloor$.
- `sum_range_mul` splits $r < kd$ as $t + kc$, and `sum_range_mul_mod` shows that $j \mapsto jc \bmod e$ permutes $\{0, \dots, e - 1\}$ for $c$ prime to $e$ (`Nat.ModEq.cancel_right_of_coprime`, `Finset.surjOn_of_injOn_of_card_le`).
- `dsum_mod`, `dsum_sub`, `dsum_inv` and `dsum_scale` prove the rules of (A). For the last one, $r = t + kc$ gives $\sum_{c<d} ((t/(dk) + c/d)) = ((t/k))$, while the second factor depends only on $t$.

### 2. Formulas.lean: integer formulas for coprime $h, k$

- `dsum_eq_sum_mul`: since $\rho(r) \ne 0$ for $0 < r < k$, $s(h, k) = \sum_r (r/k - 1/2)(\rho(r)/k - 1/2) - 1/4$. With $\sum \rho = \sum r$ this is $s(h, k) = \sum_r r\rho(r)/k^2 - (k - 1)/4$.
- `twelve_k_dsum`: with $\rho(r) = hr - kq(r)$ (`rho_eq`) and $\sum r^2 = k(k-1)(2k-1)/6$, $12 k s(h, k) = 2h(k-1)(2k-1) - 12 S_2 - 3k(k-1)$.
- `twelve_h_S2`: $\sum \rho^2 = \sum r^2$ expands to $12 h S_2 = (h^2 - 1)(k-1)(2k-1) + 6kQ$. `two_Sq`: $\sum \rho = \sum r$ gives $2\Sigma q = (h-1)(k-1)$.
- `h_twelve_k_dsum`: hence $12hk\,s(h, k) = h^2 + 1 + kM$ with the integer $M = (h^2+1)(2k-3) - 6Q - 3h(k-1)$, since $(k-1)(2k-1) = 1 + k(2k-3)$.

### 3. Reciprocity.lean

- `floor_eq_card`: for $t < h$, $\lfloor kt/h \rfloor$ counts the $r$ with $1 \le r < k$ and $hr < kt$, because $hr = kt$ is impossible for coprime $h, k$.
- `two_S2_swap`: summing $t$ over these pairs in the other order gives $2 S_2(k, h) = (k-1)h(h-1) - (Q + \Sigma q)$, because for fixed $r \ge 1$ the admissible $t$ run from $q(r) + 1$ to $h - 1$.
- `reciprocity`: a linear combination of the identities of section 2 for $(h, k)$ and $(k, h)$ with `two_S2_swap` gives $12hk(s(h, k) + s(k, h)) = h^2 + k^2 + 1 - 3hk$.

### 4. ModEight.lean: theorem 3

- `mod_eight`: strong induction on the odd modulus $k$, with $k = 1$ as base. Replace $h$ by $x = h \bmod k$, so $0 < x < k$ (`jacobiSym.mod_left`).
- For odd $x$, `mod_eight_odd_step` combines reciprocity, the induction hypothesis $12x\,s(k, x) \equiv x + 1 - 2J(k \mid x) \pmod 8$ and Jacobi reciprocity (`jacobiSym.quadratic_reciprocity_one_mod_four`, `jacobiSym.quadratic_reciprocity_three_mod_four`). In `ZMod 8` the integer $N = 12k\,s(x, k)$ satisfies $xN = x^2 + k^2 + 1 - 3xk - k(x + 1 - 2J(k \mid x))$. Since $x^2 = 1$ in `ZMod 8`, the finite table `table8` gives $N = k + 1 - 2J(x \mid k)$ there.
- For even $x$, $k - x$ is odd, $s(x, k) = -s(k - x, k)$ and $J(x \mid k) = \chi_4(k) J(k - x \mid k)$ (`jacobiSym.neg`), so the odd case and a check modulo $8$ for $k \equiv 1, 3 \pmod 4$ finish.

### 5. Criteria.lean: theorems 2, 4 and 5

- `integral` (theorem 2): for $d = \gcd(h, k)$, `dsum_scale` reduces to the coprime pair $(h/d, k/d) = (h', k')$. In `twelve_k_dsum` for this pair, $k'(k'-1)$ is even and $(k'-1)(2k'-1)$ is divisible by $3$ when $3 \nmid k'$. This gives explicit integers for $6k\,s(h, k)$ when $3 \mid k$ and for $2k\,s(h, k)$ otherwise.
- `dsum_eq_zero_iff` (theorem 4): if $s(h, k) = 0$, then $h^2 + 1 = -kM$. Conversely, if $k \mid h^2 + 1$, then $k - (h \bmod k)$ is an inverse of $h$ modulo $k$, so `dsum_inv` and `dsum_sub` give $s(h, k) = -s(h, k)$.
- `dsum_eq_iff` (theorem 5): if $s(a, k) = s(b, k)$, the two identities of `h_twelve_k_dsum` combine to $(a - b)(ab - 1) = k(aM_b - bM_a)$ (`dsum_mul_eq`), and `Int.Prime.dvd_mul'` finishes for a prime $k$. The converse is `dsum_mod` or `dsum_inv`.

### 6. Knopp.lean: theorem 1

- `sum_saw_mul`: with $g = \gcd(r, d)$, $d = ge$ and $r = gr'$, the values $br/d = br'/e$ for $b < d$ run $g$ times through the residues modulo $e$, so $\sum_{b<d} ((y + br/d)) = g\,((ey))$.
- `sum_dsum_shift`: hence $\sum_{b<d} s(x + bk, dk) = \sum_{r<dk} ((r/(dk)))\, g(r)\, ((e\, xr/(dk)))$.
- `sum_by_gcd`: grouping $r$ by $g = \gcd(r, d)$ and writing $r = gr'$ gives $\sum_{g \mid d} g\, C(d/g, x)$, where $C(e, x)$ is the sum of $((r/(ek)))((xr/k))$ over $r < ek$ prime to $e$.
- `coprimeSum_eq`: the indicator of $\gcd(r, e) = 1$ is $\sum_{f \mid \gcd(r, e)} \mu(f)$ (`ArithmeticFunction.moebius_mul_coe_zeta`). Swapping the sums and `dsum_lift` (a form of `dsum_scale`) give $C(e, x) = \sum_{f \mid e} \mu(f) s(xf, k)$.
- `sum_pairSet`, `sum_pairSet_left`, `sum_pairSet_right`: double divisor sums are sums over the pairs $(a, b)$ with $ab \mid N$, in either order.
- `collapse`: $\sum_{e \mid N} \sum_{f \mid e} \mu(f) A((N/e)f) = A(1)$, because the pair $(f, e/f)$ turns the sum into $\sum_{j \mid N} A(N/j) \sum_{f \mid N/j} \mu(f)$.
- `knopp`: with $A(m) = s(mh, k)$ the left side becomes $\sum_{g \mid n} g \sum_{e \mid n/g} \sum_{f \mid e} \mu(f) A((n/(ge)) f) = \sum_{g \mid n} g\, s(h, k) = \sigma(n) s(h, k)$ (`ArithmeticFunction.sigma_one_apply`).
