### Overview

The zip is a complete Lake project. `lakefile.toml` and `lake-manifest.json` use the pinned Mathlib checkout at `/opt/mathlib4` (commit `9944fe2973b8dc0b86949101ed98232c07cd54a0`), and `lean-toolchain` is `leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1`. The proof sits in eight files under `TuringTask/` that import each other in a chain: `Quad`, `Count`, `Euclid`, `Rings`, `Types`, `Orders`, `Main`, `Basic`. Every file starts with `import Mathlib`. The required file `TuringTask/Basic.lean` is the file of the prompt with one added line `import TuringTask.Main`. Its namespace and its five statements stay as in the prompt, and each `sorry` became a one-line `exact` of the matching theorem in `Main.lean`.

In this text $R = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with $\omega^2 = a + b\omega$ is Mathlib's `QuadraticAlgebra Int a b`. The norm of $x + y\omega$ is $N = x^2 + bxy - ay^2$, and $D = b^2 + 4a$. Three rings occur: $R_1$ with $(a, b) = (-1, -1)$, norm $x^2 - xy + y^2$ and $D = -3$, $R_3$ with $(a, b) = (-2, 0)$, norm $x^2 + 2y^2$ and $D = -8$, and $R_4$ with $(a, b) = (-2, 1)$, norm $x^2 + xy + 2y^2$ and $D = -7$. For $n \ge 1$ the number $\mathrm{cnt}(n)$ counts the pairs in the box of the prompt with norm $n$, and $S_\chi(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \chi(d)$. The characters are those of the prompt: $\chi_1(d) = J(d \mid 3)$, $\chi_3(d) = J(-2 \mid d)$ and $\chi_4(d) = J(d \mid 7)$.

The route (Dirichlet's formula through unique factorization):
- (A) Each of $R_1, R_3, R_4$ is Euclidean for the norm, so irreducible elements are prime.
- (B) Every rational prime $p$ splits as $p = \pi\bar\pi$ with $\pi \nmid \bar\pi$ when $\chi(p) = 1$, ramifies with $\pi \mid \bar\pi$ when $\chi(p) = 0$, and stays prime when $\chi(p) = -1$. The Jacobi symbol $J(D \mid p)$ decides this, and tables match it with $\chi(p)$.
- (C) For a prime $p \mid n$ the counts satisfy $\mathrm{cnt}(n) = 2\,\mathrm{cnt}(n/p) - \mathrm{cnt}(n/p^2)$, $\mathrm{cnt}(n/p)$ or $\mathrm{cnt}(n/p^2)$ in the three cases (with $\mathrm{cnt}(n/p^2) = 0$ when $p^2 \nmid n$). The divisor sums satisfy the same recursions, so induction gives $\mathrm{cnt}(n) = w \, S_\chi(n)$ with $w = 6, 2, 2$ units. This proves theorems 1, 3 and 4.
- (D) Theorems 2 and 5 concern the orders $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-7}]$ of index 2. Explicit maps compare their counts with those of $x^2 + xy + y^2$ and $x^2 + xy + 2y^2$ for $n$ odd, $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and $4 \mid n$, and the recursions at $p = 2$ turn the results into the stated sums.

### 1. Quad.lean: norms, the box, and divisor sums

- `norm_mk`, `four_norm` ($4N = (2x + by)^2 - Dy^2$), `four_a_norm`, `nm_nonneg`, `eq_zero_of_norm_eq_zero`, `mul_left_cancel'`: for $D < 0$ the norm is positive definite, so $R$ has no zero divisors.
- `in_box`: for $-2 \le a$ and $D \le -3$, a solution of $N = n \ge 1$ has $|x|, |y| \le n$, because $3y^2 \le 4n$ and $3x^2 \le 8n$. So the box of the prompt contains every solution.
- `box`, `cnt` and `cnt_dvd`: if $N(c) = k$ and $k \mid n$, then $w \mapsto cw$ is a bijection from the solutions for $n/k$ onto the solutions for $n$ that $c$ divides.
- `divSum`, `divSum_split`, `divSumT_div`, `divSum_rec`: for a completely multiplicative $\chi$ and a prime $p \mid n$, splitting the divisors by $p \mid d$ gives $S(n) = T(n) + \chi(p) S(n/p)$, where $T$ sums over the divisors prime to $p$ and $T(n) = T(n/p)$. The three recursions of (C) follow.

### 2. Count.lean: the recursions for the counts

- `intCast_norm` ($N(z) = z\bar z$), `star_dvd_of_dvd_star`, `dvd_or_dvd_star`: if $\pi$ is prime and $N(\pi) = p \mid N(z)$, then $\pi \mid z$ or $\bar\pi \mid z$.
- `cnt_split`: the solutions divisible by $\pi$ and those divisible by $\bar\pi$ cover all solutions. Both together hold exactly for multiples of $p$, because $\bar\pi \mid \pi$ would contradict $\pi \nmid \bar\pi$. With `Finset.card_union_add_card_inter` and `cnt_dvd` this gives the split recursion.
- `cnt_ram`: for $\pi \mid \bar\pi$ every solution is a multiple of $\pi$. `cnt_inert`: for $p$ prime in $R$ every solution is a multiple of $p$, and no solution exists when $p^2 \nmid n$.
- `cnt_eq`: strong induction on $n$ with $p$ the least prime factor gives $\mathrm{cnt}(n) = \mathrm{cnt}(1)\, S_\chi(n)$.

### 3. Euclid.lean: Euclidean division and primes of the ring

- `rnd`, `rnd_spec`: a nearest integer to $p/n$. `norm_rem_mul`: $N(x - yq) N(y) = N(x\bar y - N(y) q)$, so the remainder bound reduces to the two coordinates of $x\bar y - N(y)q$.
- `euclidOf`: a rounded quotient with $N(x - yq) < N(y)$ makes `QuadraticAlgebra Int a b` a Euclidean domain. It is a definition used with `letI` (instances are not allowed), and `prime_of_irreducible` reads off that irreducible elements are prime.
- `irreducible_of_norm_prime`, `prime_or_norm`: an element of prime norm is irreducible. A rational prime $p$ is either the norm of some $\pi$ or prime in $R$, because a factorization $p = xy$ into non-units forces $N(x) = p$.
- `not_dvd_star`: if $N(\pi) = p$ and $p \nmid D$, then $\pi \nmid \bar\pi$. From $\pi \mid \bar\pi - \pi = y(b - 2\omega)$ the norms give $p \mid Dy^2$, so $p \mid y$, then $p \mid x$, and $p^2 \mid N(\pi)$ is impossible.
- `jacobi_of_norm` and `exists_norm_of_jacobi`: for an odd prime $p \nmid D$, some $\pi$ has $N(\pi) = p$ exactly when $J(D \mid p) = 1$. One direction uses $4p = (2x + by)^2 - Dy^2$ with $p \nmid y$. For the other, a square root $s$ of $D$ modulo $p$ gives $z = x + \omega$ with $2x + b \equiv s$, so $p \mid N(z)$ but $p \nmid z$, and `prime_or_norm` finishes.

### 4. Rings.lean and Types.lean: the three rings

- `quo2` rounds both coordinates. `bound1` and `bound3` show $A^2 - AB + B^2 < n^2$ and $A^2 + 2B^2 < n^2$ for $|2A|, |2B| \le n$, so `hIP1` and `hIP3` give irreducible-implies-prime in $R_1$ and $R_3$. For $R_4$ that box is too large, so `quo4` rounds the second coordinate first and then $2A + B$. That gives $4N = (2A + B)^2 + 7B^2 \le \frac{11}{4}n^2 < 4n^2$ and `hIP4`.
- `jac1`, `jac3`, `jac4`: with `jacobiSym.mod_right`, tables over $p \bmod 12$, $32$ and $28$ show $J(-3 \mid p) = J(p \mid 3)$, $J(-8 \mid p) = J(-2 \mid p)$ and $J(-7 \mid p) = J(p \mid 7)$, each equal to $\pm 1$, for the odd primes $p$ prime to $3$, $2$ and $7$.
- `type1`, `type3`, `type4`: every prime has the type of (B). The ramified primes are $3 = N(1 - \omega)$ in $R_1$, $2 = N(\sqrt{-2})$ in $R_3$ and $7 = N(1 - 2\omega)$ in $R_4$, each with an explicit unit $u$ such that $\bar\pi = \pi u$. In $R_4$ the prime $2 = N(\omega)$ splits, and in $R_1$ the prime $2$ is inert because $x^2 - xy + y^2 = 2$ has no solution (`norm_ne_two_R1`).

### 5. Orders.lean: the five counts

- `cnt1`, `cnt3`, `cnt4`: `cnt_eq` with the types and the unit counts $6, 2, 2$, which `decide` evaluates on the box for $n = 1$. `card1_eq` matches the prompt's $x^2 + xy + y^2$ with the norm of $R_1$ by $(x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)$.
- `f5_odd`, `f5_two`, `f5_four`: for odd $n$ every solution of $x^2 + xy + 2y^2 = n$ has $y$ even, and $(X, Y) \mapsto (X - Y, 2Y)$ is a bijection from the solutions of $X^2 + 7Y^2 = n$. For $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ there is no solution of $X^2 + 7Y^2 = n$. For $4 \mid n$ every solution has $X \equiv Y \pmod 2$, and $(X, Y) \mapsto ((X - Y)/2, Y)$ matches them with the solutions of $x^2 + xy + 2y^2 = n/4$.
- `f2_two`, `f2_four`: the same two steps for $X^2 + 3Y^2$ and $x^2 + xy + y^2$.
- `f2_odd`: for odd $n$ the solutions of $x^2 + xy + y^2 = n$ split into the parity classes $(\text{odd}, \text{even})$, $(\text{even}, \text{odd})$ and $(\text{odd}, \text{odd})$. The rotation $(x, y) \mapsto (-y, x + y)$ and its square map the first class onto the other two, and $(x, y) \mapsto (x - y, 2y)$ maps the solutions of $x^2 + 3y^2 = n$ onto the first class. So the first count is three times the second.

### 6. Main.lean and Basic.lean

- `sum_filter_four`: $\sum_{d \mid n,\ 4 \mid d} f(d/4)$ equals $\sum_{e \mid n/4} f(e)$ when $4 \mid n$ and $0$ otherwise. `filter_odd_even_odd`, `filter_odd_even_even`: for odd $n$ no divisor $d$ has $n/d$ even, and for even $n$ the divisors with $d$ odd and $n/d$ even are the odd divisors.
- `main_one`, `main_three`, `main_four`: `cnt1`, `cnt3`, `cnt4` in the form of the prompt.
- `main_two`: $\chi_1(2) = -1$, so $S(n) = 0$ for $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and $S(n) = S(n/4)$ for $4 \mid n$. With `f2_odd`, `f2_two` and `f2_four` the count is $2S(n)$, $0$ and $6S(n/4) = 2S(n) + 4S(n/4)$ in the three cases.
- `main_five`: $\chi_4(2) = 1$. For even $n$ the second sum of the prompt is $S(n) - S(n/2)$ (`divSum_split`), and $S(n) = 2S(n/2) - S(n/4)$ (with $S(n/4) = 0$ unless $4 \mid n$). With `f5_odd`, `f5_two` and `f5_four` both sides agree.
- In `Basic.lean` each of the five theorems is one `exact` of the matching theorem.

### Sources and related work

The formulas for the three maximal orders go back to P. G. L. Dirichlet, Recherches sur diverses applications de l'analyse infinitesimale a la theorie des nombres, Journal fur die reine und angewandte Mathematik 19 (1839), 324-369, and 21 (1840), 1-12 and 134-155. The forms $x^2 + 3y^2$ and $x^2 + 7y^2$ appear in L. Lorenz, Bidrag til tallenes theori, Tidsskrift for Mathematik (3) 1 (1871), 97-114, and in N. A. Hall, The number of representations function for binary quadratic forms, American Journal of Mathematics 62 (1940), 589-598. Expositions are in B. C. Berndt, Number Theory in the Spirit of Ramanujan (AMS 2006), chapter 3, and L. E. Dickson, History of the Theory of Numbers, volume III (1923), chapter IV. The write-up and the Lean code are my own.

The proof uses these Mathlib results: `QuadraticAlgebra` with its norm and conjugation, `EuclideanDomain` (through `letI`) and `Irreducible.prime`, `QuadraticAlgebra.isUnit_iff_norm_isUnit`, `jacobiSym.mod_right`, `jacobiSym.mul_left`, `jacobiSym.mul_right'`, `ZMod.isSquare_of_jacobiSym_eq_one`, `legendreSym.eq_one_iff`, `Finset.card_union_add_card_inter`, `Finset.card_bij'` and `Nat.minFac_prime`. Mathlib at the pinned commit has `GaussianInt` and the Jacobi symbol, but no counting formula for these forms. RamanujanLean (tomdif) proves Jacobi's two-square and four-square formulas $r_2$ and $r_4$ by $q$-series. The forms here are different and the route here is algebraic. I found no Lean formalization of the five statements in Mathlib, `TauCetiProject/TauCeti`, `AllenGrahamHart/FormalConjectures-Bench`, formal-conjectures, compfiles, PutnamBench, miniF2F, ProofNet, `provables/sequencelib` or `int-y1/proofs`, or by web search (checked 10 Oct 2026).
