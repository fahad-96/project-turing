### Overview

The zip is a complete Lake project. `lakefile.toml` and `lake-manifest.json` use the pinned Mathlib checkout at `/opt/mathlib4` (commit `9944fe2973b8dc0b86949101ed98232c07cd54a0`), and `lean-toolchain` is `leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1`. The proof is split into four files under `TuringTask/` that import each other in a chain: `Lucas`, `Residues`, `Equations`, `Basic`; every file starts with `import Mathlib`. The required file `TuringTask/Basic.lean` is the file of the prompt: `import Mathlib`, the namespace and the five statements are unchanged, and each `sorry` is replaced by a one-line call of the matching theorem in `Equations.lean`.

Notation: $F_n$ is `Nat.fib n`, extended to all integers by Mathlib's `Int.fib` ($F_{-n} = (-1)^{n+1} F_n$); $L_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n+1}$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ (Lean: `lucZ n`), which for natural $n \ge 1$ is the prompt's expression `Nat.fib (n - 1) + Nat.fib (n + 1)` (Lean: `lucNat n`). So $L_0 = 2$, $L_1 = 1$, $L_3 = 4$, $L_6 = L_{-6} = 18$, $F_{\pm 1} = 1$, $F_{\pm 3} = 2$.

The route (J. H. E. Cohn's method, with integer indices):
- (A) Shift congruences: for even $k$ and odd $t$, $F_{m + 2kt} \equiv -F_m$ and $L_{m + 2kt} \equiv -L_m \pmod{L_k}$ for every integer base $m$.
- (B) If $k$ is even and $3 \nmid k$, then $L_k \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, so $L_k$ has a prime factor $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and $L_k$ never divides $x^2 + c^2$ when $\gcd(c, L_k) = 1$ (used with $c = 1, 2, 3$, i.e. $x^2 \equiv -1, -4, -9$).
- (C) Every integer $N > 0$ with $4 \mid N$ is $N = 2kt$ with $k$ even, $3 \nmid k$ and $t = 3^e$ odd ($k \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ when $8 \mid N$). Hence any index $n$ that is not one of the exceptional values is $n = m + 2kt$ with a small base $m \in \{0, \pm 1, \pm 3, \pm 6\}$, and (A) and (B) give a contradiction.
- (D) Even indices: $F_{2m} = F_m L_m$ with $\gcd(F_m, L_m) \mid 2$, so a square or twice a square $F_{2m}$ forces $L_m$ to be a square, twice a square or four times a square, which reduces theorems 1 and 3 to theorems 2 and 4 at $m$. Theorem 5 follows from theorems 1 and 3 by the parity of the exponent.

### 1. Lucas.lean: Lucas numbers with integer index and the shift congruences

- `lucZ`, `fib_neg_of_even` ($F_{-k} = -F_k$ for even $k$), `fib_one_sub_of_even` ($F_{1-k} = F_{k-1}$ for even $k$), both from `Int.fib_neg`.
- `fib_add_add_fib_sub`: $F_{a+k} + F_{a-k} = F_a L_k$ for even $k$ and every integer $a$: expand both terms with Mathlib's `Int.fib_add` ($F_{m+n} = F_{m-1} F_n + F_m F_{n+1}$) and use the two sign rules; the $F_{a-1}$ terms cancel.
- `lucZ_add_add_lucZ_sub`: $L_{a+k} + L_{a-k} = L_a L_k$, the previous identity at $a - 1$ and $a + 1$ added.
- `lucZ_dvd_fib_shift_pow`, `lucZ_dvd_lucZ_shift_pow`: $L_k \mid F_{m + 2kt} - (-1)^t F_m$ and $L_k \mid L_{m + 2kt} - (-1)^t L_m$, by induction on $t$ (apply the identities at $a = m + 2kt + k$).
- `lucZ_dvd_fib_shift`, `lucZ_dvd_lucZ_shift`: the odd case, $L_k \mid F_{m+2kt} + F_m$ and $L_k \mid L_{m+2kt} + L_m$.
- `lucZ_natCast`: for natural $n \ge 1$, `lucZ n` is the prompt's expression.

### 2. Residues.lean: residues, the gcd, and the obstruction modulo L(k)

- `fib_mod_eq_of_period`: if $F_P \equiv 0$ and $F_{P+1} \equiv 1 \pmod M$, then $F_n \bmod M$ depends only on $n \bmod P$ (from `Nat.fib_add`, $F_{n+P} = F_n F_{P-1} + F_{n+1} F_P$ and $F_{P-1} \equiv 1$); used with $(M, P) = (2, 3), (3, 8), (4, 6), (8, 12)$.
- `lucNat`, `lucNat_mod`, and the residue facts, each checked on the residue classes by `decide`: `lucNat_mod_four_of_even` ($L_k \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ for $k$ even, $3 \nmid k$), `lucNat_mod_four_even` ($L_n \equiv 2$ or $3 \pmod 4$ for even $n \ge 1$), `lucNat_mod_three_ne_zero` ($3 \nmid L_k$ when $4 \mid k$), `lucNat_mod_eight_odd` ($L_n \not\equiv 0, 2 \pmod 8$ for odd $n$), `fib_mod_two` ($F_n$ is even iff $3 \mid n$).
- `lucNat_eq` ($L_n = 2F_{n-1} + F_n$), `lucNat_mod_two` ($L_n \equiv F_n \pmod 2$), `fib_two_mul_eq` ($F_{2m} = F_m L_m$, from `Nat.fib_two_mul`), `gcd_fib_lucNat_dvd_two` ($\gcd(F_m, L_m) \mid 2$, since it divides $2F_{m-1}$ and $F_{m-1}, F_m$ are coprime by `Nat.fib_coprime_fib_succ`).
- `exists_prime_dvd_mod_four`: $M \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ has a prime factor $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ (strong induction: if the least prime factor is $\equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the cofactor is again $\equiv 3 \pmod 4$).
- `not_dvd_sq_add_sq`: if $M \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ and $\gcd(c, M) = 1$, then $M \nmid x^2 + c^2$: modulo such a prime $p$, $c$ is invertible and $(x/c)^2 = -1$, which `ZMod.exists_sq_eq_neg_one_iff` excludes for $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
- `exists_split_three`: $N = 2kt$ as in (C), from `Nat.exists_eq_pow_mul_and_not_dvd` with the prime $3$; $k$ is even (and divisible by $4$ when $8 \mid N$) because $3^e$ is coprime to $2$ and $4$.
- `lucZ_not_dvd`, `lucZ_not_dvd_sq_add_one`, `lucZ_not_dvd_sq_add_four`, `lucZ_not_dvd_sq_add_nine`: $L_k \nmid x^2 + 1$, $x^2 + 4$ ($L_k$ odd), and $x^2 + 9$ when $4 \mid k$ ($3 \nmid L_k$).

### 3. Equations.lean: the five classifications

- Helpers: `sq_mod_four`, `two_mul_sq_mod_eight`, `lucZ_dvd_fib_nat` and `lucZ_dvd_lucNat` (the shift congruences for a natural index written $n = m + 2kt$), `lucZ_dvd_of_dvd_two_mul` (cancel a factor $2$, $L_k$ odd), `split_of_mod_four`, `sq_of_coprime_mul` (coprime factors of a square are squares, `exists_eq_pow_of_mul_eq_pow`).
- `lucNat_eq_sq_cases` (theorem 2): even $n$ is excluded modulo $4$. For $n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $n \ne 1$: $n = 1 + 2kt$, so $x^2 = L_n \equiv -L_1 = -1 \pmod{L_k}$. For $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $n \ne 3$: $n = 3 + 2kt$, so $x^2 \equiv -L_3 = -4$. Both contradict (B).
- `lucNat_eq_two_mul_sq_cases` (theorem 4): odd $n$ is excluded modulo $8$. For $n \equiv 0 \pmod 4$: $n = 0 + 2kt$, $2x^2 \equiv -L_0 = -2$, so $x^2 \equiv -1$. For $n \equiv 6 \pmod 8$, $n \ne 6$: $n = 6 + 2kt$ with $4 \mid k$; for $n \equiv 2 \pmod 8$: $n = -6 + 2kt$ with $4 \mid k$; in both cases $2x^2 \equiv -18$, so $x^2 \equiv -9 \pmod{L_k}$ with $3 \nmid L_k$.
- `fib_odd_index_dvd`: odd Fibonacci indices with base $m \in \{1, -1, 3, -3\}$, $n \equiv m \pmod 4$, $n \ne m$.
- `fib_lucNat_halves` ($3 \mid m$: $F_m = 2a$, $L_m = 2b$, $\gcd(a, b) = 1$) and `fib_lucNat_odd_coprime` ($3 \nmid m$: both odd and coprime).
- `fib_eq_sq_cases` (theorem 1): odd $n \ne 1$: base $1$ or $-1$, $x^2 \equiv -1$. Even $n = 2m$, $m \ge 1$: if $3 \nmid m$, $L_m$ is a square, so $m \in \{1, 3\}$ by theorem 2, hence $m = 1$; if $3 \mid m$, $x = 2y$ and $ab = y^2$, so $L_m = 2b$ is twice a square and $m = 6$ by theorem 4.
- `fib_eq_two_mul_sq_cases` (theorem 3): odd $n \ne 3$: base $3$ or $-3$, $2x^2 \equiv -2$, so $x^2 \equiv -1$. Even $n = 2m$: $3 \nmid m$ makes $F_m L_m$ odd; for $3 \mid m$, $x = 2y$ and $ab = 2y^2$. If $b$ is even, $L_m = 4s^2$ is a square and theorem 2 gives $m = 3$; if $b$ is odd, $L_m = 2s^2$, theorem 4 gives $m = 6$, and $F_{12} = 144 = 2x^2$ is impossible ($72$ is not a square).
- `fib_eq_two_pow_cases` (theorem 5): $2^{2j} = (2^j)^2$ and $2^{2j+1} = 2(2^j)^2$, then theorems 1 and 3; $F_0 = 0$ and $F_{12} = 144$ are not powers of $2$.

### 4. Basic.lean

The file of the prompt; each theorem is proved by one call: `fib_eq_sq_cases`, `lucNat_eq_sq_cases`, `fib_eq_two_mul_sq_cases`, `lucNat_eq_two_mul_sq_cases`, `fib_eq_two_pow_cases` (the prompt's Lucas expression is `lucNat n` by definition).

### Sources and related work

The four square and twice-square classifications are due to J. H. E. Cohn, On square Fibonacci numbers, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 39 (1964), 537-540, and J. H. E. Cohn, Square Fibonacci numbers, etc., The Fibonacci Quarterly 2 (1964), 109-113; theorem 1 was proved independently by O. Wyler, American Mathematical Monthly 71 (1964), 220-222. The powers of two among Fibonacci numbers are a classical consequence (and a special case of the perfect-power classification of Y. Bugeaud, M. Mignotte and S. Siksek, Annals of Mathematics 163 (2006), 969-1018). The proofs here follow Cohn's method of shift congruences modulo $L_k$ with integer indices and a power-of-3 splitting of the index; the write-up and the Lean code are my own.

Related Lean code: Mathlib at the pinned commit has `Nat.fib` and `Int.fib` with the addition and doubling formulas, `fib_neg`, `gcd_fib` and Cassini's identity, but no Lucas numbers and none of the five statements. I found no Lean formalization of these classifications in Mathlib, TauCetiProject/TauCeti, AllenGrahamHart/FormalConjectures-Bench, compfiles, PutnamBench, formal-conjectures or by web search (checked 9 Oct 2026). One of the model responses to this task proves the statements along a different arrangement of Cohn's method (a power-of-2 splitting of the index with Jacobi symbols, as its README states); the golden answer was written from the statements and the Mathlib sources without reading that proof, and an automated scan found no shared declaration names and no shared lines apart from the prompt's statements.

The golden answer is one valid route, not the only one. Any complete proof of the five statements is acceptable.
